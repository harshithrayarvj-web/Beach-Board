# *Designing the models for the keyboard*

## *Importing parts and making a screen support (8/6/2026)* - (5 Hours)
I imported model into onshape, but the PCB didn't import cleanly and it lagged onshape, so I only selected parts for one key (diode and hotswap) and also the pico and mcp.
I also imported an mx switch model, stabilizer models, and 2 models for my 1.8 inch screen (so I could compare them, they had the same dimensions but different sized mounting holes).
During this stage I put the stabilizers where they should be, and also moved switches to them to check if they lined up, I found out that my stabilizers were placed rotated 180 and was thankfully
not a mounting hole problem. Placing the switches and stabilizers verified the plate that was generated by a plate generator, which I had to spend around 30 minutes to an hour to create my same keyboard digitally for it.
After I finished verifying the plate, I made the 1.8inch screen support which held onto a single mounting hole as I didn't want to risk making it hold onto another hole above, especially as the space available
wasn't enough to be confident in a 3D print (I've had a few bad experiences with a 3D printer at my public library, but I ended up going with somewhat tight models because I thought I could
trust the printing legion).
The reason why I chose a support instead of mounting holes was because I wasn't sure about where the holes would fit, and I wanted to prioritize where ethe pins were placed more than the mounting points.
